Global Halal Supply Chain Limited, based in Hong Kong, is the international operating and digital-infrastructure vehicle for a programme called AMANAH — Global Halal Digital Trust. Its purpose is to connect the evidence surrounding a product with the organisations, controls and accountable decisions that shape the product’s journey from origin to market. AMANAH is the operational experience. The Amanah Halal Trust Ecosystem, or AHTE, provides the standards, evidence, orchestration and trust-intelligence framework that supports that experience.

International Halal trade already depends on certificates, laboratories, auditors, manufacturers, logistics companies, importers and public authorities. The difficulty is that each organisation may hold a different part of the record. A certificate alone is no longer sufficient to explain every relevant question: which product and production batch it covers, which evidence supports it, who handled the goods, what changed during transit and which authority made the decision required at the destination.

AMANAH connects those records around a continuing product identity. It is to carry appropriate context between participants, preserve who supplied each record and make the next responsible action easier to determine. In this way, evidence can be considered alongside the physical movement of the goods, rather than being separated from it in disconnected files and transactions.

The operating model begins with an organisation and its authorised representatives. A manufacturer’s identity, facility, production lines, products and stock-keeping units can be brought into scope together with suppliers, ingredients and raw materials. Each relationship has a defined context: a supplier provides a material; a facility produces a product; a batch links production to the goods that will later be sampled, stored, shipped and received.

Evidence is attached to the object and event it supports. A document, observation, test result or custody record is considered with its source, date, scope and integrity information. Where a required record is missing, expired, inconsistent or outside its permitted scope, the system can make that gap visible for review. A record is not made authoritative simply because it has been uploaded or assigned a high confidence score.

Standards and authority instruments are then applied to the relevant product, process, facility and market. The Malaysian and JAKIM framework includes applicable Malaysian Standards together with certification manuals, halal management requirements, protocols, circulars, authority instructions, destination rules and laboratory methods. Applicability determines which requirements matter; it is not reduced to a single certificate check or a permanently fixed list.

Laboratory work follows the sample from request and identification through collection, seal, transfer, transport and receipt. The record continues through accession, method, quality control, result, technical review, authorised signatory and report. Linking those steps to the product and batch helps a reviewer assess what the result means and what it does not mean. NOT DETECTED does not mean Halal; laboratory evidence supports assessment but does not itself grant certification.

Audit connects written controls with what happens at the facility; an auditor may use a tablet or smart glasses to view the relevant requirement, inspect a process, record an observation and attach attributable evidence. AI assists. Humans and competent authorities decide. The system can retrieve controls, identify missing records and organise notes, while the human auditor remains responsible for judgement and conclusions; a finding leads to corrective action, evidence of completion and re-verification before the next authorised gate is considered.

Evidence before trust. Trust before operational release. Authority before certification.

These words describe the sequence of the system. Evidence is gathered and assessed; trust states describe the resulting record and its limits; operational release remains a separate controlled state; and certification remains with the authority empowered to make that decision.

Where certification or another reserved decision is required, the workflow routes the case to authorised people through the defined authority process. AHTE’s target connectivity is AHTE ⇄ Direct JAKIM API ⇄ JAKIM. Any formal status must come from the authorised decision process and its evidence. AI can describe, diagnose, predict, prioritise and recommend, but cannot create a certification decision or bypass a reserved authority gate.

Production records connect the approved product and process to a batch. Sensors and operational systems can contribute relevant condition, time, location and equipment events, while a digital twin keeps the physical facility, line, goods and records aligned. If a reading, identity or process event conflicts with an applicable control, the event can be examined and escalated. No score should conceal a failed hard requirement or silently authorise release.

The same identity continues through storage and movement. Warehouse activity can record receiving, segregation, location, condition and dispatch. Sinotrans logistics can be represented through accountable custody handoffs, vehicle and route events, container and seal references, and relevant environmental conditions. Each transfer records who released the goods, who received them and what evidence accompanies the transfer.

At the origin port and customs boundary, the system can organise pre-arrival information, shipment identity and inspection evidence for the authorised parties. It can carry the authorised response into the journey, but it cannot make a sovereign border decision. International transit preserves the relationship between the shipment, its batch, container, seal and custody history while events continue to be reconciled against their permitted sources.

The China to GCC route is direct. In the GCC, port and customs processes remain under the relevant sovereign authority. The importer’s workflow continues with onboarding, licences, pre-arrival records, receiving checks, container and seal reconciliation, condition review, discrepancy handling and quarantine where required. Destination inventory remains traceable to its source, and an importer can assign eligible lots to the next accountable distributor.

A distributor or third-party logistics provider records receipt, storage, stock rotation where applicable, custody transfer, delivery and exception handling. Retailers and marketplaces can assess listing eligibility, product and batch mapping, receiving, stock status, verification, withdrawal and recall. If a problem arises, the affected batches and downstream locations or orders can be identified for accountable review and action, rather than treating the supply chain as if it ended at the port.

A buyer, retailer, authority or consumer sees only the information authorised for that audience. A product disclosure can present identity, relevant credential status, issuing authority, selected provenance and permitted custody confirmations. It must not expose confidential supplier records or imply that a valid QR code is itself proof of Halal status. The disclosure communicates its scope and links back to evidence without confusing a token with a certification decision.

A shared Command Center can bring together the journey timeline, custody, evidence, exceptions and operational signals. Predictive analysis may identify patterns that warrant attention; a preemptive recommendation may direct a person to inspect, hold, contact or verify. The response remains attributable to the authorised role. If an exception creates a hold, quarantine, corrective action or recall path, those states are recorded and the next action can be followed through re-verification.

Evidence before trust. Trust before operational release. Authority before certification.

The principle applies at every handoff, from raw material and laboratory sample to warehouse, port, importer and consumer disclosure. A connected record helps participants see what is supported, what remains unresolved and which party owns the next decision.

AMANAH does not replace JAKIM, competent Halal authorities, laboratories, auditors, certification bodies, customs authorities, port authorities or Shariah authorities. It does not turn an analytical result, an AI recommendation, a trust score or an operational release into a Halal certificate. Shariah finance and Takaful may use appropriately scoped evidence, but their financing, underwriting, claims and Shariah decisions remain with their own authorised counterparties.

The system’s value rests on maintaining those distinctions while making the full journey easier to inspect. A manufacturer can trace a material into production; a reviewer can follow evidence to its source; a logistics operator can account for a custody transfer; an importer can investigate a receiving discrepancy; and a consumer can view an authorised disclosure. Each participant sees the context needed for its role without acquiring another organisation’s decision rights.

Where a production connector, authorised identity, mandate or external response has not been established, the platform must state that boundary accurately. A demonstration can illustrate the end-to-end operating model, but it cannot create a real shipment, laboratory appointment, authority approval, port release or commercial commitment. Real activation depends on the relevant parties, evidence, contracts, permissions and acceptance tests.

The thesis is therefore direct. Halal trade across borders needs evidence that stays connected to products, people, processes and decisions as goods move from origin to consumer. AMANAH provides a digital trust and assurance infrastructure for that continuity, while preserving the authority of each institution and the accountability of every participant.
